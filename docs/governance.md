# Governance

Roles set permission ceilings: Owner, Orchestrator, Agent, and Observer. Capabilities and repository/resource scopes narrow access. Principals are permanently classified HUMAN or AGENT; HUMAN approval cannot be supplied by an automated principal.

Granting the Orchestrator role is a hard, human-only check on top of the ordinary owner-or-orchestrator elevation `agent activate`/`agent_activate` already requires: an existing Orchestrator that is itself an AGENT principal cannot promote any principal — including itself or another agent — to Orchestrator. Only an active HUMAN principal (the owner, or any other human already holding an elevated role) may grant it. This closes an escalation path that would otherwise let one AI-driven Orchestrator mint further Orchestrators with no human ever in the loop. The check is enforced in `internal/protocol/transitions.go`, the one transition validator shared by the CLI, MCP, TUI, and daemon across both authority backends — not duplicated per interface, so it cannot be bypassed by going through a different one.

The principal-type check verifies the signer's credential, not that a human was actually, presently deciding this specific action — and it cannot be otherwise, since authority here is the signing credential, and whoever holds a credential is cryptographically that principal. If an agent operates without a distinct, self-registered identity of its own — resolving to the project owner by ambient fallback rather than deliberately switching to it — it is cryptographically HUMAN, indistinguishably from the owner acting directly. Because that alone isn't enough to guarantee a human actually decided this in the moment, granting the Orchestrator role requires a second, independent control on top of it: a pre-existing, separately-approved, HUMAN-tier approval record for that exact grant (`approval.action` == `agent.activate:<id>`), also enforced in `internal/protocol/transitions.go`. Anyone — including an agent acting under ambient fallback — may *apply* by creating the approval request (`approval request --tier HUMAN --action agent.activate:<id>`), but the grant cannot proceed until that request is separately approved (`approval approve`, e.g. a TUI keypress) at a later moment. This forces a genuine two-step process a single autonomous command cannot complete unattended, and gives you a real, visible pending request to review before anything is granted. The operational discipline still matters as defense in depth: every agent should hold its own registered identity (see docs/agent-onboarding.md); an owner-fallback connection should never be used for anything beyond bootstrapping that one self-registration, and orchestrator approvals specifically should always be approved by your own hands, never relayed through an agent even at your own request.

`agent revoke`/`agent_revoke` is the terminal counterpart to activation: once revoked, a principal can never act again (the general active-principal gate blocks every further transition) and can never be reactivated, renamed, or suspended — a permanently frozen record, not erased history. The project Owner can never be revoked, by anyone, including itself, so a project can never be left without one. Revoking an Orchestrator-role principal or any HUMAN principal requires the same human-only check that granting the Orchestrator role requires, unless a principal is revoking itself: an AI-driven Orchestrator must not be able to unilaterally strip another Orchestrator or a human of standing to entrench itself, the same escalation concern the grant-side check already closes. Revoking a plain Agent- or Observer-role principal needs only the ordinary owner-or-orchestrator elevation. Revocation never auto-cancels the target's in-flight invocations or auto-reassigns its owned tasks — `agent-comms doctor` surfaces any such orphaned work as a separate, non-blocking warning for an owner or orchestrator to resolve deliberately.

Assignments are expiring offers. Acceptance or eligible self-claim creates a four-hour protected lease. Overlapping write resources are rejected unless a governed shared-write exception exists. Heartbeats show liveness but cannot renew ownership. A renewal is durable and includes a progress summary. Stale work is never automatically reassigned. Handoffs retain ownership until acceptance.

Message obligations are per recipient: FYI delivers without acknowledgement; ACTION is accepted or rejected and then completed; CONTRACT requires all named parties; BLOCKER is acknowledged and resolved; DECISION requires read acknowledgement. Routine scoped work is autonomous. Scope changes, takeovers, shared writes, and contracts need orchestrator governance and affected acknowledgements. Destructive, irreversible, external, force-push, production-data, and credential actions require HUMAN approval.
